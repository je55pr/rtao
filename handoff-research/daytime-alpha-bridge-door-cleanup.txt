Alpha edge follow-up / corrected diagnosis:
- the earlier premultiplied-alpha explanation was not the cause of the reported bunting/foliage clipping. FLD/220 bunting TBP 10493 is strictly binary alpha (1028 opaque / 1020 transparent texels), and every transparent texel is RGB black; premultiplication therefore changes none of its source pixels. The FLD/220 bush TBP 10742 is likewise binary-alpha with black transparent RGB.
- a deterministic reproduction at FLD/220 render-local camera X1383/Z1365 shows that the golden hill visible through/behind the flags belongs to a neighbouring resident FLD. Rendering FLD/220 alone leaves literal sky behind those cards.
- persistent-world submission was still sector-local: sector A opaque -> sector A alpha -> sector B opaque -> sector B alpha. A linearly filtered low-alpha edge from FLD/220 could therefore blend against sky and write depth before the neighbouring sector's opaque hill/building was submitted, so that later geometry could not replace the sky-coloured fringe.
- the renderer now performs frame-global phases: all visible-sector opaque batches first, then all visible-sector alpha/cutout batches. Opaque and alpha billboard batches are split into the same phases; dynamic palm crowns remain in the alpha phase. This fixes the background-clipping pixels while preserving the authored linear filtering.
- PAL GS TEST_2 packets with half-alpha thresholds were investigated as a diagnostic. Forcing ReferenceAlpha=128 also hides the fringe, but it is not a valid global fix: FLD/220 TBP 10926 contains authored ground-shadow texels with alpha 64, and the half-alpha shortcut deletes those shadows. Keep the existing fully-transparent discard (Greater/0) for the current renderer.
- the prior premultiplied upload/blend path remains in this cleanup branch, but it is no longer considered the explanation or fix for the bunting/foliage clipping issue.
